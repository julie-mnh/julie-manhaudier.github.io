Festival de Cine Francés de Málaga

Vox pops

Street interviews Bilingual interviews Video editing

The concept

During the Festival de Cine Francés de Málaga, I created a series of five vox pop videos, one for each of five different films. The idea was to catch the audience right as they walked out of the screening and capture their reactions while the emotion was still fresh: what the film made them feel, what stayed with them, what they wanted to say in the moment.

The process

I teamed up with the festival's press officer, who filmed me so I could stay focused on the people while still getting the shots I needed. I knew I'd be facing a mostly Spanish-speaking crowd, so I prepared my questions in Spanish, even though my Spanish was still shaky at that point. The hardest part wasn't the language though, it was convincing strangers to step in front of the camera. Some screenings ended at 10pm or even midnight, when most people just want to go home. So I had to find a way to approach them without scaring anyone off with a mic and a camera. Then I edited each video the same night and published it the next day, so it stayed in sync with the festival program.

What I learned

Vox pop is probably the hardest format I've worked in. You have to project confidence, walk straight up to people you don't know, accept that plenty of them will say no, and keep going anyway. It pushed me well out of my comfort zone. But once you're past that first hurdle it gets easier, and the refusals stop stinging. I learned that the whole thing lives or dies in the first few seconds of an approach, in how you carry yourself before you've even asked a question.
